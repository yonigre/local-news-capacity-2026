County local-capacity bands, 2026
Backstory & Strategy, "If They Can't Make Money, How Will You?"

What this is
For each of the 3,144 U.S. counties, an estimate of how many full-time journalists a new, lean, digital-only news operation could fund from local revenue alone: what it could prudently raise from local readers, local business sponsors and local foundations, measured against a reporter's salary, benefits and payroll taxes in that state.

Bands
None | Under 1 | 1 to 2 | 2 or more

Two columns
The first includes local foundation giving. The second excludes it, because IRS data assigns a foundation's grants to the county where the foundation is registered, not where its money goes, which can inflate results in a few counties.

What it leaves out
Legal notices, revenue from an established print business, outside philanthropy and government support. These are estimates of local self-sufficiency for a newcomer, not verdicts on any existing newspaper. The model also prices reporters at the state median wage and uses county boundaries, which rarely match a local paper's actual market.

News desert and single-source categories
This file does not include them. To see whether a county is a news desert or has one local news source, use the Medill Local News Initiative's State of Local News 2026 interactive map.

Sources
Inputs: U.S. Census Bureau (American Community Survey, County Business Patterns), IRS (990-PF data), Bureau of Labor Statistics.
Analysis: Yoni Greenbaum, Backstory & Strategy.
